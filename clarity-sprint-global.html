The pattern

Does this sound familiar?

"I know the answer. In English, it comes out smaller."

"In my own language I sound like a leader. In English I sound like an engineer."

"The interview went well technically. Then they hired someone else."

"In meetings with US leadership, the moment passes before I've found the words."

You don't have a language gap. You have a translation gap.

Fluency gets you understood. Executive English gets you chosen: the decision first, the structure senior leaders expect, and stories that show judgment instead of effort. This isn't grammar or accent work. It's how leaders at global companies hear the difference between "a strong engineer" and "our next VP."

What happens

Two weeks, start to finish

  1. Day 0

    You book and send your materials

    A short intake form, plus your résumé, LinkedIn, most recent performance review, the role you're aiming for, and any interview or presentation coming up.

  2. Days 1–4

    I read everything

    I look for the decisions, scope, and impact hiding in your work: the parts experts leave out.

  3. Day 5

    90-minute Translation Session

    One deep 1:1 conversation. We find your next-level story and practice it out loud, including the interview questions you dread most.

  4. Day 8

    Your Translation Brief arrives

    A written document you keep and use in interviews, reviews, and executive meetings.

  5. Days 12–14

    30-minute follow-up

    We pressure-test the plan before you use it, and answer anything that came up.

The deliverable

What's in your Translation Brief

  1. Your one-sentence positioningHow you introduce yourself in English, and what you want decision-makers to repeat about you.
  2. The gap mapWhat the next level requires, how you're read today, and the distance between them.
  3. Three proof storiesYour strongest work, rewritten as interview-ready answers that show what you owned and decided.
  4. Rewrites and answer formulasTen line rewrites for your résumé or LinkedIn, plus the structures for interview answers and executive updates.
  5. Your pathPromotion where you are, a new company, or a new country, and the reasoning behind the call.
  6. Your 30-day planThe first five moves, in order, with what "done" looks like for each.
